## 🌌 About the Project

What if a browser did more than load websites?

What if it also encouraged you to think about *why* you opened them?

Modern browsers make accessing social media, entertainment, and other websites almost effortless. Sometimes, that convenience turns into habitual scrolling, endless feeds, and hours of distraction.

**Artemis Surf** is an independent Python-built browser project exploring a different approach: putting user control, intentional browsing, and digital well-being at the center of the experience.

The goal isn't to keep you online longer. It's to rethink how you spend your time online.

## 🧠 The Problem: Autopilot Browsing

<p align="center">
  <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bhuvanesh-m-dev/artemis-surf/refs/heads/main/img/readme-content/2.jpg" alt="Artemis Surf digital well-being concept: autopilot browsing versus intentional browsing" width="100%">
</p>

A familiar cycle:

1. Open a social media website for a quick check.
2. Encounter an endless feed.
3. Continue scrolling without a clear intention.
4. Realize more time has passed than expected.

This isn't just about social media. It's about how digital experiences can encourage automatic behavior.

Artemis Surf explores ways to make browsing more deliberate through optional pauses, configurable website-access friction, and user-controlled focus features.

**The idea: make room for conscious choices without taking control away from the user.**

## 🏗️ Conceptual Architecture

Artemis Surf can be understood through four conceptual layers:

**1. Browser Interface**

The user-facing experience, including navigation, address entry, tabs, and the start page.

**2. Web Access**

The components responsible for requesting, loading, and displaying websites.

**3. Privacy Controls**

Settings and mechanisms for managing browsing data, sessions, and retained information.

**4. Digital Well-being**

Optional controls designed to encourage intentional access to selected websites and reduce habitual distractions.

The precise rendering engine, networking stack, and supporting components depend on the actual implementation. This diagram represents the conceptual design rather than a claim about verified internal components.

## 🔄 From Autopilot to Intentional Browsing

<p align="center">
  <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bhuvanesh-m-dev/artemis-surf/refs/heads/main/img/readme-content/5.jpg" alt="Artemis Surf intentional browsing workflow: pause, choose, open, explore, and return" width="100%">
</p>

Artemis Surf explores a simple alternative to automatic browsing habits:

**PAUSE → CHOOSE → OPEN → EXPLORE → RETURN**

- **Pause:** Take a moment before entering a distracting website.
- **Choose:** Decide what you want to accomplish.
- **Open:** Access the website you selected.
- **Explore:** Browse with a clearer intention.
- **Return:** Finish when you're ready and return to your other activities.

The goal is not to block social media or dictate how people use the internet. It's to explore how optional friction and thoughtful interface design can help users make more deliberate decisions.

## 🔐 Privacy by Design

<p align="center">
  <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bhuvanesh-m-dev/artemis-surf/refs/heads/main/img/readme-content/6.jpg" alt="Artemis Surf privacy-conscious browsing design and user-controlled data retention" width="100%">
</p>

Privacy should be understandable, not assumed.

Artemis Surf explores a privacy-conscious approach to browsing, including the handling of browsing history, downloads, cookies, and session data.

Important design goals include:

- Minimize unnecessary data retention.
- Make data-handling behavior clear.
- Provide user control over retained browsing information.
- Avoid unnecessary collection of personal information.
- Document the browser's actual privacy limitations.

Not displaying a history or download list does not necessarily mean the underlying data has been deleted. Privacy guarantees depend on how the browser and its supporting components actually handle data.
